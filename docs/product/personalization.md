# Personalization Architecture

Personalization makes InvestGuide adaptive without turning it into an advisor. It changes how information is explained, ordered, taught, and compared. It must never remove user agency or create hidden steering toward financial products.

## How Personalization Works

InvestGuide personalization begins with onboarding and evolves through explicit user edits plus transparent behavioral signals.

Core profile dimensions:

* experience level
* risk appetite
* investment horizon
* planned investment range
* preferred asset types
* investment goals
* preferred language level
* education focus

Personalization affects:

* dashboard defaults
* explanation depth
* metric visibility
* education prompts
* comparison framing
* asset discovery ordering
* AI answer tone and assumptions

## Profile Evolution

User profiles should evolve through:

* direct profile edits
* onboarding updates
* saved assets and comparisons
* completed lessons
* repeated education topics
* AI questions asked
* watchlists and future portfolios

Every adaptive behavior should be reversible. Users should know why the platform is adapting.

## Beginner Experience

Beginner users need confidence, context, and definitions.

Experience traits:

* simple English
* visible glossary terms
* fewer metrics by default
* guided next steps
* examples from local markets
* stronger education prompts

AI language:

* defines jargon inline
* explains why a metric matters
* avoids compressed analyst language
* asks clarifying questions before complex answers

## Intermediate Experience

Intermediate users understand basic concepts and need tradeoff analysis.

Experience traits:

* balanced explanations
* selected advanced metrics
* sector and macro context
* comparison prompts
* deeper risk and income framing

AI language:

* explains relationships between metrics
* compares alternatives
* introduces assumptions and limitations
* links concepts to user goals

## Advanced Experience

Advanced users need depth, methodology, and concise insight.

Experience traits:

* more metrics visible by default
* methodology notes
* assumptions and data caveats
* advanced comparisons
* macro sensitivity and scenario framing

AI language:

* uses precise financial terminology
* exposes formulas and assumptions
* reduces basic definitions unless requested
* supports deeper research workflows

## Adaptive Dashboards

Dashboards should change emphasis, not truth.

Examples:

* income-focused users see dividends, distributions, REITs, and yield stability more prominently
* growth-focused users see quality, momentum, earnings context, and sector expansion
* conservative users see volatility, liquidity, capital preservation, and defensive education
* VFEX-focused users see USD exposure and currency context

## Adaptive Education

Education should follow user needs:

* beginners receive fundamentals and glossary support
* income users receive dividend and REIT education
* macro-sensitive users receive inflation and currency explainers
* advanced users receive methodology and source notes

## Adaptive Analytics

Analytics visibility should scale by experience level.

Beginner: score explanations, simple risk labels, key drivers.

Intermediate: score components, trend context, comparison tradeoffs.

Advanced: formulas, assumptions, sensitivity, raw metric panels.

## Privacy and Control

Personalization must be transparent, editable, and privacy-conscious. Users should be able to change their profile, reset adaptive signals, and understand what is used to personalize the experience.
